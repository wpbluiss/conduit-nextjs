## Long-form script (~7–9 min) — "The First World Cup Ever | 1930"

**COLD OPEN (0:00–0:15)** — hook before the title
> In 1930, thirteen teams sailed across an ocean to a country most of them had
> never seen… to play a tournament that had never existed. No one knew if it
> would work. Ninety-three thousand people showed up anyway. This is the story
> of the very first World Cup — and the one-armed striker who ended it.
*Visual: AI period shot — 1930s steamship cutting through grey ocean, sepia, film grain.*

**ACT 1 — A crazy idea (0:15–2:00)**
- FIFA president Jules Rimet's dream: one tournament for the whole world.
- Uruguay chosen as host — they were the best team on earth (back-to-back Olympic champions) and were celebrating **100 years of independence**.
- They built the **Estadio Centenario** — 90,000 seats — in a *single year*. Rain nearly stopped it being finished in time.
*Visuals: AI-built 1930s Montevideo, construction of a grand stadium, Jules Rimet portrait-style render.*

**ACT 2 — The journey (2:00–3:45)**
- Most of Europe refused — the trip was a **two-week boat ride** across the Atlantic. Only four European teams came.
- Players trained *on the deck of the ship*. Some had never left their home country.
*Visuals: AI players running drills on a ship deck, maps with animated sailing routes.*

**ACT 3 — The tournament (3:45–5:30)**
- 13 teams, no qualification — invitation only. The very first goal in World Cup history was scored by France's Lucien Laurent.
- Uruguay and Argentina — fierce rivals — both power through to the final.
*Visuals: animated original bracket/graphics, AI 1930s crowd scenes.*

**ACT 4 — The final (5:30–7:30)** — the payoff
- July 30, 1930. 93,000 fans, gates open **six hours** before kickoff. Fans searched at the turnstiles for guns.
- Two countries, one river between them, so heated they used **a different ball for each half**.
- Argentina lead 2–1 at halftime. Uruguay storm back: **4–2.** The final goal scored by **Héctor Castro — who had lost part of his arm as a boy.**
- The next day was declared a **national holiday**. Uruguay were the first world champions.
*Visuals: AI-recreated packed 1930s stadium, dramatic slow push-ins, a trophy reveal.*

**OUTRO + loop (7:30–end)**
> They sailed across an ocean for a tournament that might have failed — and gave
> us the biggest event on Earth. Next: the World Cup that was stolen. Literally.
> Someone walked out with the trophy. Subscribe.
