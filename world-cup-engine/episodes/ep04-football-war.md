# Episode 04 — "The World Cup Game That Started a War" (1969)

**Bucket:** Rivalries / Scandals. **Length:** ~6–8 min. **Why it works:** "a football
match caused a real war" is an irresistible curiosity gap. Handle responsibly — the
matches were the *flashpoint*, not the root cause.

## Long-form narration script
> **HOOK:** In the summer of 1969, two countries played a World Cup qualifier. Within
> weeks, their armies were firing on each other. Thousands would die. The newspapers
> called it "The Football War." But the truth is darker — football was just the spark
> that lit a fire that had been building for years.
>
> El Salvador and Honduras: two small, crowded Central American neighbors. For years,
> tensions had simmered — over land, over jobs, over hundreds of thousands of Salvadoran
> migrants living in Honduras. The two nations were a powder keg. And then they were
> drawn to play each other to reach the 1970 World Cup.
>
> The first match, in Honduras, turned ugly — fans kept the visiting team awake all
> night before the game. Honduras won. The second, in El Salvador, was worse. By the
> third deciding match, the violence had spilled far beyond the stadium. Riots. Border
> clashes. Both countries cut ties.
>
> **(THE TURN)** On July 14th, 1969, it stopped being about football entirely. El
> Salvador's army crossed the border. For roughly 100 hours, the two nations were at
> war — which is why it's also called the **Hundred Hour War**. By the time a ceasefire
> held, around two thousand people were dead and tens of thousands were displaced.
>
> Football didn't *cause* the war — poverty, land, and politics did. But it was the
> match that struck the spark. It remains the only time in history a path to the World
> Cup ran straight through a battlefield.
>
> **CLIFFHANGER:** That war was bigger than one man. Our next story is about one man,
> one own goal, and the price he paid.

## 5 Shorts
1. **"A World Cup qualifier that started a REAL war"** — the premise.
2. **"Why it's called the 100-Hour War"** — the duration hook.
3. **"They kept the other team awake all night before the match"** — the dirty tactics.
4. **"Football didn't cause the war — but it lit the match"** — the nuance/twist.
5. **"The only road to the World Cup that ran through a battlefield"** — the closer.

## Fact-check + sensitivity note
1969 El Salvador–Honduras "Football War" / "100 Hour War"; WC 1970 qualifiers were the
flashpoint; underlying causes were socioeconomic (migration, land). ~2,000+ dead. Keep
the framing respectful and factually careful — real deaths, not entertainment. Verify
figures before narration.
